# AstralBridge Expedition

## Question

What is the least machinery required to make repeated bounded exchanges between this browser apparatus and an independently instantiated Google AI Mode conversation cheap enough that transport stops dominating the interaction?

The desired loop is:

**apparatus → outside model → apparatus**

The crossing may encounter browser, product, permission, or security boundaries. Discover those boundaries through evidence rather than assuming them in advance.

## Starting condition

AstralBridge already contains a functioning browser executable inherited from a general-purpose substrate. It also contains a proven model-operated build, immutable candidate, preview, exact-byte promotion, and GitHub Pages deployment path.

Use whatever inherited machinery is useful. Remove whatever is not.

The existing world is not sacred.

## Interaction requirement

A human using AstralBridge should be able to conduct repeated exchanges with Google AI Mode while doing as little clerical transport work as practical.

The apparatus should make clear:

- what is intended to cross outward;
- what actually returns;
- where a crossing begins and ends.

Preserve enough exact evidence of crossings that later inspection can distinguish sent material from returned material.

The human may remain an unavoidable physical bridge across a browser or provider boundary. If so, make that role small, obvious, and fast.

## Evidence from the first real run

The first published crossing candidate has now been exercised through ten completed crossings with a continuing Google AI Mode conversation.

The preserved run is in `evidence/crossings-001.json`, with the immediate finding beside it in `evidence/crossings-001.md`.

That run establishes a baseline:

- manual outward-and-return transport works;
- the ledger preserves each outward message and return;
- conversational continuity survived repeated crossings, including sparse continuation with `➡️`;
- state and consequence accumulated across the external conversation;
- the complete run took 12 minutes 41 seconds;
- the last six crossings took a median 19.4 seconds from beginning to recorded return, including Google response time and human handling;
- even a tiny continuation still requires the full copy, switch, paste, send, wait, copy, switch, paste, record routine;
- the apparatus currently records the exchange but contributes no evolving state or behavior to it.

More narrative turns through the same interaction are not useful evidence for the present bottleneck. The next executable work should materially reduce human transport burden.

Do not optimize merely for removing an arbitrary click or one nominal manual step. Optimize for the experienced crossing becoming substantially less clerical.

## Payload requirement

The useful crossing is not limited to short text messages.

Future crossings must remain compatible with carrying large heterogeneous bodies of evidence: structured and unstructured text, images, screenshots, data, executable or inspectable artifacts, archives, and combinations of these.

The external product may expose useful transport affordances such as file attachments or archive handling. Investigate actual available behavior rather than assuming current limits or capabilities, and treat those affordances as candidate mechanisms rather than architecture.

Do not build a generalized multimedia transport system merely because one can be imagined. But do not reduce present text friction in a way that creates a local optimum hostile to rich payloads.

A crossing may ultimately be better understood as transferring a bounded parcel of evidence rather than merely sending a text message. That possibility is investigative freedom, not a required design.

## Investigative freedom

Investigate the actual browser and Google AI Mode behavior available to the human.

Do not assume in advance that the correct mechanism is a clipboard, another tab, a deep link, an API, browser automation, a share surface, manual transfer, or any other particular implementation.

Do not build infrastructure merely because it could someday be useful.

Make ordinary engineering, interaction, and presentation decisions yourself. Prefer the smallest reliable mechanism supported by executable evidence.

If a more automated crossing is brittle, unavailable, unsafe, or disproportionately expensive, stop at the cheapest reliable human-mediated crossing and make that crossing excellent.

Specificity is allowed. This expedition does not need to solve every browser, device, model provider, or future use case.

## Deliberate non-commitments

This expedition does not assume or require:

- persistence;
- memory;
- identity;
- personality;
- a character;
- an agent architecture;
- orchestration;
- a generalized model-provider interface;
- autonomous conversation;
- a durable conversation-history system;
- a particular meaning for the external model beyond being the other side of this crossing.

Do not introduce these concepts unless executable necessity during this expedition earns them.

## Working method

Proceed through executable evidence.

Inspect the current implementation. Investigate external constraints where necessary. Implement the smallest promising crossing. Run it. Use it. Diagnose friction and failure. Simplify or replace machinery that does not earn its cost.

Preserve successful behavior while iterating, but do not preserve failed approaches out of attachment to previous work.

Use the inherited candidate and preview machinery during development. The human should evaluate executable interaction, not repository plumbing.

Do not stop at a plan or a static mockup.

## Finish line

The expedition is complete when:

1. AstralBridge supports repeated bounded outward-and-return exchanges with Google AI Mode through a reliable interaction the human can actually use.
2. Human clerical work is low enough that the crossing itself no longer feels like the experiment.
3. The apparatus preserves inspectable evidence of what was sent and what returned.
4. The implementation contains no substantial machinery that exists only for an imagined future architecture.
5. The resulting candidate has been exercised repeatedly enough to expose obvious interaction friction and material failure modes.
6. The Semantic Surface has been updated to describe the truths of the resulting code rather than its inherited starting point.

If product boundaries prevent a fully automatic round trip, that is not failure. A minimal, reliable, fast human-mediated seam satisfies the expedition if further automation would cost more than it earns.

## Return

When the finish line is reached, return:

- the live candidate for human inspection;
- a short account of the crossing that actually worked;
- the important boundaries or failures discovered;
- what machinery survived because it proved necessary;
- what tempting machinery was deliberately not built.

Do not promote the candidate merely because it works technically. Promotion follows human acceptance of the experienced preview.
