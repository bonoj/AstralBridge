# Crossing 001

Ten real crossings were completed against the published AstralBridge candidate built from `62f05e3b8ba7e686d336dddfeb8cef7f4df250e7`.

The exported ledger content is preserved in [crossings-001.json](./crossings-001.json).

## What the run established

The crossing was usable. A continuing Google AI Mode conversation maintained enough trajectory for sparse continuation, including a bare `➡️`, to remain coherent. Earlier state could be recalled and changed, and actions accumulated consequences across multiple turns.

The run also exposed the present limit of the apparatus.

After ten crossings, the experiment was stopped because the human-mediated transport remained laborious enough to dominate the interaction. The human was still acting as packet router across surfaces: copy, switch, paste, send, wait, copy, switch, paste, record, repeat.

During the run, conversation naturally began routing around the ledger surface because discussing the returned material elsewhere was cheaper than continuing to operate every part of the apparatus.

## Earned pressure

The evidence does not yet earn a provider API, browser automation, generalized abstraction, agent loop, or persistence architecture.

It does earn a narrower question:

> What is the smallest reliable reduction in human transport work that makes repeated crossings cheap enough for the crossing itself to recede from attention?

The mechanism remains deliberately unspecified.
